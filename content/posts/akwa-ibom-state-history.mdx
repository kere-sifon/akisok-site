For those of us from Akwa Ibom living abroad, our state's origins are a reminder of how recent yet pivotal this part of our history truly is. On September 23, 1987, what had been the southwestern territories of Cross River State were carved out to establish Akwa Ibom as an independent state. The creation wasn't arbitrary—it brought together four distinct administrative divisions: Uyo, Ikot Ekpene, Eket, and Abak, which had previously been organized under the old Calabar province.

This consolidation of these four divisions into a single state laid the foundation for what Akwa Ibom has become today. The state now comprises 31 Local Government Areas, each representing communities with their own distinct identities, languages, and cultures. Understanding this administrative structure helps diaspora members appreciate how governance is distributed across our homeland and why each LGA remains important to our collective identity.

For diaspora members, this moment in 1987 marks the beginning of Akwa Ibom's journey as a distinct political and administrative entity. It's the birth year from which we measure our state's modern development, achievements, and challenges. Whether you're from Uyo, Ikot Ekpene, Eket, Abak, or any of the other LGAs that followed, this date connects us to a shared origin story that defines us as Akwa Ibom people.
