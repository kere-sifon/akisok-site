For those of us watching Akwa Ibom from afar, there's something quietly transformative happening back home. Governor Umo Eno's administration has been systematically building the digital infrastructure that could reshape our state's economic future—and frankly, it's worth paying attention to.

The moves are strategic: a submarine cable landing that connects us directly to global internet networks, the AKeID digital identity system that's establishing a foundation for everything from financial services to governance, and platforms like AkwaRemit that recognize the reality of our diaspora—we send money home, and we deserve efficient, dignified systems to do it.

What strikes me about this approach is that it's not just about flashy tech announcements. There's also investment in skills centres, which means our young people aren't just consuming technology; they're learning to create and innovate within it. That's the difference between a state that uses the digital economy and one that builds it.

For the diaspora, this matters. Better digital infrastructure means stronger business possibilities, easier connections to family, and most importantly, it signals that leadership is thinking about how Akwa Ibom competes in a world where geography matters less than digital connectivity.

The question now isn't whether we have the right direction—it's whether we can sustain the momentum and ensure these systems actually reach the people they're designed to serve. That's always the gap between vision and reality. Still, for once, it feels like someone at home is asking the right questions about our future.
