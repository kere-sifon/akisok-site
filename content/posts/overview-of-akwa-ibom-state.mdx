For those of us from Akwa Ibom scattered across the world, there's a particular pride in knowing our state's origin story. Three decades ago, our homeland emerged as its own entity when it was carved out from Cross River State on September 23, 1987. That moment marked not just a political separation, but the beginning of a distinct identity for millions of Akwa Ibomites.

What's often forgotten in that founding narrative is the deliberate choice of Uyo as our capital. It wasn't chosen by accident or convenience—leadership at the time made a conscious decision to position Uyo in a way that could catalyze development across every corner of the state. The thinking was strategic: by placing the seat of government there, they hoped to create opportunities that would ripple outward to all our local government areas and communities.

Today, as we reflect on nearly 35 years of statehood, it's worth asking ourselves how well that vision has held up. Has development truly spread equitably across our state, or have certain areas thrived while others have been left behind? For the diaspora especially, these questions matter because they touch on what home means to us—what we're investing in, what we're hoping to build, and what we want for those still there.

Our state's founding was meant to be transformative. Whether we've lived up to that promise is a conversation every Akwa Ibom person, wherever they are, should be having.
