There's something deeply moving about watching our ancestral traditions come alive in spaces where the diaspora can witness them. A recent cultural showcase at the Christmas village in Uyo captured the vibrancy of Etim Ekpo heritage—the intricate masquerade performances, the dignified traditional regalia, and the Uto Ekpe customs that have defined our identity for generations.

For those of us scattered across the globe, these documented moments become precious threads connecting us back home. The careful display of our cultural expressions reminds us that there's a living, breathing tradition waiting for us beyond the screens and the distance. The Christmas village setting is particularly poignant—a space where celebration and cultural pride intersect, where our people gather to honor who we are.

What strikes me most is how these performances aren't frozen in time; they're evolving expressions of our heritage. The youth participating, the crowds gathering, the stories being told through every mask and every step—this is culture not as museum piece but as lived experience.

If you've been away for years, or if you've never experienced these traditions in person, this documentation is a window. And if you're planning a trip home, this is exactly the kind of cultural immersion that feeds the soul. Akwa Ibom's cultural landscape is rich, and initiatives like the Christmas village showcase remind us why maintaining these connections matters—both for those of us abroad and for the generations growing up at home.
