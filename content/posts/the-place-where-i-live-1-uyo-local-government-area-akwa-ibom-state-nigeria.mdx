There's something deeply grounding about revisiting the cultural markers of home, especially when you're far away. Uyo Local Government Area holds a treasure trove of traditions that many of us grew up around—some we've carried with us, others we're only now beginning to fully appreciate from the distance of diaspora living.

The unwang ofong isin, that distinctive loincloth, represents more than just clothing; it's a thread connecting us to our ancestors and to each other. And then there's the food—afang soup and edikang ikong aren't just dishes, they're embodied memories. Anyone who's tasted these delicacies knows that no restaurant abroad can quite replicate the exact flavor of home, that particular way our mothers and grandmothers prepared them with intention and time.

What strikes me most is how these cultural expressions—the ekpo masquerade with all its mystique and the abang dance with its infectious rhythm—create moments where the entire community comes together. These aren't relics of the past; they're living, breathing parts of who we are as Akwa Ibom people. For those of us scattered across different continents, holding onto these cultural threads becomes a way of holding onto identity itself.

Revisiting stories like this reminds us that while we may have physically left Uyo, these cultural practices remain part of our inheritance. They're worth knowing deeply, sharing with the next generation, and returning to celebrate whenever we get the chance to come home.
