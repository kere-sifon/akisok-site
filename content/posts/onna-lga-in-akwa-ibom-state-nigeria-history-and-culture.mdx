For those of us from Onna, there's something deeply grounding about understanding where we come from. Our local government area comprises 48 distinct communities, each with its own character and heritage. Many who've lived abroad or spent time elsewhere may recall the historical designation—when Onna was administratively bundled with Eket, outsiders often spoke of us collectively as "Onna-Eket," a naming that reflected our interconnected past.

Today, Onna stands as a significant oil and gas producing region within Akwa Ibom State, a reality that has shaped much of our contemporary identity and economic landscape. Yet beyond the industrial markers, what defines us is the tapestry of our villages—48 distinct settlements that preserve our cultural threads, our languages, our ways of being.

For the diaspora, reconnecting with this history matters. It grounds us in something tangible. Understanding that we're not simply "from Akwa Ibom" but specifically from Onna—with our particular history, our villages, our contributions to the state—strengthens our sense of belonging, even from afar. Whether you're in Lagos, London, or New York, knowing these details about home helps us carry our identity with intention.

The history of Onna is still being written by those of us who remember it and those discovering it anew. Each village holds stories. Each family carries narratives. Reconnecting with these details is how we stay rooted, regardless of where we've planted ourselves in the world.
