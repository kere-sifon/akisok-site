There's something stirring in Uyo that deserves your attention, especially if you're part of the Akwa Ibom diaspora. An inaugural Diaspora Day conference is bringing our scattered community back home—at least in spirit and conversation—to discuss what we can collectively contribute to the state's development.

This gathering, organized under the ASACOP umbrella, signals a shift in how Akwa Ibom is thinking about its relationship with those of us spread across Nigeria and beyond. Rather than viewing the diaspora as people who've simply moved on, the state is recognizing us as active partners in progress.

What makes this particularly intriguing is the focus on the ARISE Agenda—a development framework that goes beyond rhetoric. The conference isn't just a celebration; it's a working session where diaspora members will hash out real partnership opportunities with the home government. These are conversations that matter: how remittances translate into strategic investments, how skills and expertise from abroad can address local challenges, how we stay connected to development priorities even from a distance.

For those of us in the diaspora, this feels like an invitation to be heard. Too often, we're positioned as sources of funding without genuine input into vision and strategy. This conference suggests something different—a recognition that building Akwa Ibom requires integrating the knowledge, networks, and resources we've accumulated elsewhere.

Whether you're planning to attend in person or simply following from abroad, this inaugural event marks a moment worth paying attention to. It's about reshaping the diaspora narrative from one of departure to one of partnership.
