If you've got a startup that's already moving—customers are using your product, your team is grinding daily, and you're building from Akwa Ibom—this could be your moment.

Akwa Ibom Tech Week 2026 is opening its main stage in Uyo to founders like you. This isn't a theoretical pitch competition where people talk about ideas that might happen someday. They're looking for the real thing: businesses with traction, with evidence that people actually want what you're building.

The platform alone is worth showing up for. You'll be pitching directly to investors who write cheques, corporate leaders scouting partnerships, and an engaged audience of other tech builders. But the incentive is hard to ignore: ₦10 million on the line, and crucially, it comes without strings attached—no equity required.

For those of us scattered across diaspora spaces, this feels like a rare moment. Akwa Ibom's tech ecosystem is growing, and the state is actively creating spaces where founders can access capital and visibility without leaving home. If you've been wondering whether building in the state gives you real access to resources, this competition is a signal that the infrastructure is forming.

Whether you're considering a return to build, or you're already embedded and growing something locally, applications are open. The bar is high—they want live products and real customers—but that's actually encouraging. It means the state is serious about supporting sustainable businesses, not just flashy pitches.

This is one of those moments worth taking seriously.
