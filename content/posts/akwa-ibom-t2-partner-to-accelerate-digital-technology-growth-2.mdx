Governor Umo Eno is making strategic moves to position Akwa Ibom as a tech-forward state. His recent partnership with T2, the telecommunications company formerly known as 9mobile, signals serious commitment to digital infrastructure and innovation across our homeland.

This collaboration isn't just about better network coverage—it's about creating the ecosystem where tech talent, startups, and digital enterprises can thrive. For those of us in the diaspora watching how our state develops, this kind of partnership matters. It means young people back home have better tools to build businesses, innovate, and participate in the global digital economy without having to relocate.

The South-South region has enormous untapped potential in the tech space. With the right partnerships and investments, Akwa Ibom could become a regional hub that attracts entrepreneurs and tech professionals. This is the kind of foundational work that takes years to show results, but when it does, it transforms economic opportunities for entire communities.

For diaspora members considering investments or tech ventures back home, these developments are worth monitoring. A state investing in digital technology infrastructure creates pathways for remote work, digital businesses, and knowledge transfer from the diaspora back to local communities. It's the kind of smart governance that recognizes where the future economy is heading.

The real measure of success will be in the outcomes—how many young Akwa Ibom people can build careers in tech, how many startups emerge, and whether this translates into concrete opportunities. But the direction is encouraging.
