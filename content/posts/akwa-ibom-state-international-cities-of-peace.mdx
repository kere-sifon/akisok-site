Akwa Ibom has earned recognition as an International City of Peace, a designation reflecting the state's commitment to building a culture of reconciliation and non-violence across communities. The recognition highlights the diverse peace-building work happening across the state—from grassroots interfaith initiatives that bring religious communities together, to structured campaigns tackling violence prevention in schools and beyond.

What stands out is the emphasis on restorative approaches rather than punishment. Schools and community centers across Akwa Ibom are increasingly turning to healing-focused programs that address the root causes of conflict, helping both victims and perpetrators find paths toward reconciliation. This includes comprehensive anti-bullying efforts and violence prevention campaigns that recognize young people as agents of change.

For those of us in the diaspora, this recognition offers a glimpse of the Akwa Ibom we might not always hear about—one where communities are actively choosing dialogue over division, where interfaith partnerships are strengthening social bonds, and where institutions are reimagining justice as transformation rather than retribution. It's a reminder that progress toward peace is often quiet, local, and sustained by countless individuals and organizations working at the grassroots level.

The International Cities of Peace designation isn't merely ceremonial; it reflects real work being done to shift how we think about conflict, community safety, and justice itself.
