Abak deserves far more attention than it gets. Nestled in the heart of Akwa Ibom, this town is home to the resourceful Annang people and tells a story that many of us in the diaspora have perhaps overlooked or simply never knew existed beyond family gatherings.

A recent travel documentation takes viewers through the real Abak—not the glossy, curated version, but the authentic pulse of daily life. You see the vibrant energy of local markets where commerce flows with a rhythm all its own, vendors calling out their wares, and the kind of organic economic activity that keeps communities thriving. It's the backbone of most Nigerian towns, yet it's rarely the subject of widespread conversation.

What strikes you watching this is how much development is quietly happening in spaces we don't always highlight. Infrastructure, commerce, community life—they're all evolving in ways that contradict the narrative of stagnation we sometimes carry about our hometowns when we're abroad.

For those of us in diaspora, there's something humbling about seeing our roots through this lens. Abak isn't waiting for international validation; it's moving forward with the energy of its people. Whether you're from the town itself, have family connections there, or simply want to understand Akwa Ibom beyond the capital, this is a perspective worth exploring.

It's a reminder that the real stories of our state—the ones that actually matter—are often found in these quieter places that deserve their moment in the spotlight.
