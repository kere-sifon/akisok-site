Governor Umo Eno has extended a compelling invitation to Akwa Ibom people living abroad—particularly those in the United States—to become active ambassadors for their homeland. Rather than viewing the diaspora as disconnected from home, the governor sees them as valuable assets with unique leverage: the professional expertise, international networks, and financial capacity that can fundamentally transform how the world perceives Akwa Ibom.

The call speaks to something deeper than mere economic interest. Tourism and cultural preservation are interconnected. When diaspora members champion our heritage—whether through business partnerships, cultural events, or investment in hospitality infrastructure—they're not just creating revenue streams. They're ensuring that younger generations, both at home and abroad, understand the richness of Akwa Ibom's traditions, history, and people.

For many in the diaspora, this represents an opportunity to bridge two worlds. Those who've built careers internationally can apply those skills toward projects at home. A finance professional abroad might structure tourism investment; a chef might establish restaurants celebrating Akwa Ibom cuisine; entrepreneurs might develop cultural heritage sites. The possibilities extend far beyond what government alone can achieve.

What makes this particularly timely is the global hunger for authentic cultural experiences. Travellers increasingly seek genuine encounters with local communities and traditions, not just standardized resort experiences. Akwa Ibom has that authenticity in abundance—our festivals, arts, architecture, and warmth are genuinely distinctive.

For the diaspora, responding to this call isn't charity; it's strategic self-interest. Building Akwa Ibom's tourism and cultural economy strengthens the very communities many of us call home, creates opportunities for relatives, and positions our state competitively in a global marketplace increasingly valuing cultural tourism.

Governor Eno's message essentially says: your connections matter. Your ambition matters. Come home, not just to visit, but to invest—in culture, in tourism, in our future.
