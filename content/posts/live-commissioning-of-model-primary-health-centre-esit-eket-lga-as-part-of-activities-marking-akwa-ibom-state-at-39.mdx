Esit Eket has a reason to celebrate beyond the state's 39th anniversary milestone. A brand-new Model Primary Health Centre has just been commissioned in the LGA, marking a significant step forward in healthcare accessibility for residents. The facility represents the kind of infrastructure investment that communities in the diaspora often hope to see back home—modern, well-equipped spaces where locals can access quality primary healthcare without having to travel to distant urban centers.

For those of us watching from abroad, projects like this hit differently. We remember the frustrations of seeking basic medical services, the long journeys to Uyo or Itam when someone fell ill. A model health centre in Esit Eket changes that reality for families still living there. It's the sort of tangible development that reminds us why we maintain our ties to home, why we invest in our communities' futures.

The commissioning comes as part of the state's 39th-year celebrations—a moment when Akwa Ibom is taking stock of progress made and projecting where it's headed. Healthcare infrastructure is foundational. You can't talk about genuine development without addressing how people access medical care. This centre, by serving as a "model," suggests it could also set a standard for similar facilities across other communities.

Whether you're based in Lagos, London, or the United States, news like this from your home LGA carries weight. It's proof that leadership is thinking beyond ceremonial gestures. Real infrastructure that improves daily life—that's what sticks. Esit Eket residents now have a healthcare facility that reflects modern standards, and that's worth celebrating with them.
