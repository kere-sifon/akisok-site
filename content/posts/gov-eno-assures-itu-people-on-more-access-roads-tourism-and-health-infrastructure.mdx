Governor Eno has made fresh commitments to Itu Local Government, pledging that his administration will accelerate infrastructure development across the area. The focus areas include expanding road networks to improve connectivity, investing in tourism infrastructure to unlock the LGA's economic potential, and strengthening healthcare facilities to bring better medical services closer to residents.

For many of us in the diaspora with ties to Itu, this resonates deeply. We've watched our hometown navigate the challenges of limited access roads and inadequate health infrastructure for years. Better road networks mean easier movement for our families back home, safer travel, and improved commerce. Tourism development could position Itu as a destination, creating opportunities for businesses and employment that might even pull some of us back home.

The healthcare infrastructure commitment is particularly significant. A well-equipped local health system reduces the burden on residents who would otherwise travel long distances for quality medical care. It's a foundational piece for community development that we often overlook when discussing economic progress.

What makes this noteworthy is whether these assurances translate into concrete action. Infrastructure promises require sustained funding, transparent implementation, and accountability. As we follow these developments from abroad, the real measure will be seeing these projects move from announcement to completion. The diaspora community often becomes a resource—whether through investment, expertise, or advocacy—when government initiatives align with community needs.

Itu's development trajectory matters to all of us who call it home, even from a distance.
