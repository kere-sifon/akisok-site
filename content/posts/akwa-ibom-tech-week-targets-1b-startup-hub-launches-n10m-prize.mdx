Akwa Ibom is making a serious statement about its ambitions in the tech economy. The state's fifth iteration of its annual Tech Week is signaling that it's no longer content with just participating in Nigeria's startup conversation—it wants to incubate a company that will eventually command a billion-dollar valuation from its home base.

What's particularly telling is the commitment reflected in the money on the table. By doubling down with a N10 million total prize pool and raising the top award to N5 million, organisers are sending a clear message: they're willing to back their vision with resources. For an emerging tech ecosystem outside the traditional hubs, this kind of financial commitment can be the difference between watching talent migrate elsewhere and actually retaining and nurturing homegrown innovation.

For those of us from Akwa Ibom scattered across the world, there's something energising about seeing our state take this calculated risk on technology and entrepreneurship. It reflects a recognition that the future economy won't be built entirely in Lagos or Abuja—that regional tech hubs with genuine commitment and resources can compete for the next generation of breakthrough ideas.

The question now is whether the prize structure and publicity will be enough to attract the calibre of innovators who have the potential to build something transformative. But the message is unmistakable: Akwa Ibom isn't waiting to import solutions. It's laying the groundwork to create them.
