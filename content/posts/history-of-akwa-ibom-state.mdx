Our state carries within it a recent but significant chapter of Nigeria's administrative history. Akwa Ibom emerged as a distinct entity in 1987, carved out from what was then Cross River State during a period of national reorganization. What began as a new political boundary has evolved into something far more consequential on the national stage.

Today, Akwa Ibom stands as Nigeria's premier hydrocarbon powerhouse. The state's subsurface holds reserves that have fundamentally shaped the nation's economy and positioned us as a critical player in global energy markets. This wealth, however, represents more than just statistics—it reflects the potential and strategic importance of our land and people.

Home to over five million residents, our state has grown into a significant demographic center in the South-South region. From Uyo, our capital, governance and development initiatives ripple outward to touch communities across all corners of the state. The capital itself has transformed over the decades, evolving from a quieter settlement into a bustling administrative hub that increasingly reflects our state's aspirations.

For those of us in the diaspora, understanding this trajectory—from 1987 to now—helps ground our sense of place and identity. We come from a state that, while young in formal political terms, has accumulated considerable influence and responsibility. Our collective challenge remains ensuring that the wealth and potential beneath our soil translates into tangible prosperity and opportunities for all our people, regardless of where they call home.
