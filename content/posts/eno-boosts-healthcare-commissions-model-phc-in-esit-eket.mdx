In a move that reflects the administration's commitment to strengthening healthcare infrastructure in rural communities, Akwa Ibom State Governor Umo Eno has commissioned a Model Primary Health Centre in Esit Eket. This facility represents a significant step toward democratizing access to quality medical services across the state, particularly for residents in areas that have historically struggled with adequate healthcare provisions.

The establishment of this modern PHC signals a broader recognition that primary healthcare forms the backbone of any functional health system. By investing in such facilities at the grassroots level, the state government is addressing a critical gap that has long plagued healthcare delivery in underserved local government areas. For the diaspora community watching from abroad, such infrastructure projects often serve as tangible proof that development is reaching home—and that public health remains a priority despite Nigeria's myriad challenges.

The Esit Eket facility, designed to serve as a model, likely incorporates contemporary standards in equipment, staffing, and service delivery protocols. This approach not only improves immediate health outcomes for the local population but potentially establishes benchmarks that other communities can aspire toward. It's the kind of incremental progress that, multiplied across multiple LGAs, could meaningfully transform healthcare access in Akwa Ibom State.

For those of us in the diaspora, such developments are reminders of why engagement with home matters—not just emotionally, but practically. When our state invests in foundational services like healthcare, it strengthens the institutions and infrastructure that support our families back home and make return migration more feasible.
