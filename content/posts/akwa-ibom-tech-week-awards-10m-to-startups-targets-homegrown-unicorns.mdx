Akwa Ibom is making a serious bet on homegrown innovation. During Tech Week, the state distributed ₦10 million in awards to promising startups—a tangible signal that policymakers recognize the region's potential as a technology hub. But beyond the cash prizes, what stands out is the broader vision: connecting local entrepreneurs with investors and global markets.

These events matter more than they might appear. They're not just about celebrating tech founders; they're infrastructure for an ecosystem that's still finding its footing. When you bring together entrepreneurs, investors, and policymakers under one roof, something shifts. Ideas that seemed local suddenly look scalable. Connections that wouldn't happen organically in Lagos suddenly become possible in Uyo.

For those of us in the diaspora, this is worth watching. The best opportunities aren't always overseas. Akwa Ibom's tech movement represents something deeper—a generation deciding to build home rather than export their ambition elsewhere. The ₦10 million might seem modest compared to what Silicon Valley deploys, but it's the right signal at the right time.

Whether this leads to actual unicorns or simply strengthens the baseline of viable startups remains to be seen. But the infrastructure—the network, the validation, the capital—is being built intentionally. That's progress worth keeping an eye on.
